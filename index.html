Synthesis Writing Check: Whose Promise Was Kept?

This is a standalone, ~20-minute writing check — separate from the rest of today's lesson. Your response here is graded, not a practice run.
The Question: Compare the Exodusters and the Chinese railroad workers using these three lenses:
  1. Economic treatment / pay
  2. Legal status and rights
  3. Safety and violence
Based on these three, whose promise came closer to being kept?

What you need to write for (hover the 🔎 criterion name to see what a strong (4-level) response looked like when we studied Reconstruction — this is meant to show you the SHAPE of strong writing you've already seen, not something to copy):

  • Thesis/Claim — a clear, arguable answer to the question, not just a restatement of it.
  • Contextualization — set up the historical situation BEFORE your claim.
  • Use of Evidence — cite at least one specific source from EACH thread (Exodusters and Chinese railroad workers).
  • Analysis and Reasoning — explain HOW your evidence supports your claim, not just that it exists.
  • Vocabulary Use — use at least 2 Tier 2 and 4 Tier 3 words correctly.
A common problem: most students can write a claim, but skip context entirely and jump straight into it. Before you write your claim, spend 1–2 sentences answering: what was already happening before this decision that makes it make sense? (Example: Reconstruction's collapse and the rise of debt-based sharecropping; or the CPRR's labor shortage.) This goes at the START of your Claim box, not somewhere else.

Defining a term as part of your sentence counts as using it correctly — for example: "Singleton was recruiting through labor recruitment, the practice of actively persuading people to relocate for work." That's a valid use of the term, not a weaker one.

Tier 2 words — use at least 2 (hover for definitions):

push factor, pull factor, perspective, corroborate, evidence, reasoning, contrast, discrimination, counter-argument.

Tier 3 words — use at least 4 (hover for definitions):

Exoduster, nativism, sojourner, naturalization, Chinese Exclusion Act, labor recruitment.
